\begin{abstract}
Traditional symbolic regression (SR) engines often fail to converge when discovering physically meaningful algebraic corrections due to catastrophic cancellation at asymptotic limits, and they generally lack explicit mechanisms to report when observational data is insufficient to justify a new structural hypothesis. We introduce the Asymptotic Dictionary Correction Discovery (ADCD) framework, a deterministic, correction-first approach that reconstructs physical corrections atop known classical theories. By strictly regularizing primitive functions to vanish at the classical limit and utilizing deterministic grammar enumeration, we eliminate the reliance on stochastic gradient-based structure search and mitigate numerical precision collapse. Coupled with a rigorous four-step validation protocol incorporating Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) sweeps, ADCD explicitly reports structural identifiability. We do not claim general-purpose discovery; rather, we validate our framework on specific scenarios within confirmed identifiability regimes, demonstrating that constrained, regularized enumeration provides highly reproducible structural reconstructions while actively rejecting unsupported hypotheses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The evolution of physical laws often proceeds not from a \textit{tabula-rasa} vacuum, but through asymptotic corrections to well-tested classical baselines. For instance, the transition from Newtonian mechanics to Einstein's special relativity did not discard classical momentum; rather, it introduced a structural correction (the Lorentz factor) that seamlessly reduces to the Newtonian baseline at low velocities. This ``correction-first'' paradigm is central to how physicists theoretically extend established laws into new observational regimes. 

However, applying general-purpose symbolic regression (SR) to find these asymptotic corrections directly from raw observational data is notoriously difficult. Tabula-rasa algorithms, which attempt to reconstruct the entire law from scratch (often relying on stochastic genetic programming), are highly susceptible to experimental noise. They frequently overfit, producing algebraically complex expressions that fail to respect known classical limits. 

More critically, general SR paradigms typically struggle with \textit{identifiability}. When the observational data does not sufficiently deviate from the classical regime, multiple structurally divergent equations can yield near-identical empirical errors. We have observed that in widely used SR tools, the structural recovery is often acutely sensitive to hidden hyperparameters, such as parsimony pressure, leading to ``whiplash'' behaviors where recovery success wildly oscillates based on minor tuning.

In this work, we present a \textit{correction-first} formulation. ADCD (Asymptotic Dictionary Correction Discovery) is a deterministic framework for reconstructing and validating physical corrections to classical theories, using algebraically regularized asymptotic primitives and an explicit validation protocol that detects when data is insufficient to identify the correct structure. ADCD is not proposed as a general-purpose replacement for SR engines like PySR or AI Feynman; it is a specialized verification-and-reconstruction instrument designed for scenarios where the mathematical structure must obey strict physical asymptotes.

\section{Related Work}
Recent advancements in physics-inspired SR (e.g., PySR, AI Feynman, PhySO) have successfully utilized deep learning, reinforcement learning, and evolutionary algorithms to recover physical equations from data. While powerful for unconstrained discovery, they generally lack an explicit, built-in statistical reporting mechanism to declare when a dataset is theoretically unidentifiable---often returning a highly confident but structurally incorrect equation when noise dominates the physical signal.

For model selection and identifiability, classical statistics provides robust tools such as the Fisher Information Matrix and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). We acknowledge that ADCD does not invent a new statistical metric; rather, it rigorously integrates the established BIC (using the Kass-Raftery scale) into a four-step validation protocol specifically tailored for structural physics corrections, elevating identifiability from a post-hoc analysis to a gatekeeping mechanism.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Correction-First Formulation}
Rather than predicting the target observable $y_{obs}$ from scratch, ADCD assumes a known classical baseline $y_{classical}$ and searches exclusively for a correction term $\Delta$. For multiplicative corrections, $\Delta = (y_{obs} / y_{classical}) - 1$. The framework operates on a dimensionless parameter $u$, defined such that the classical limit strictly corresponds to $u \to 0$.

\subsection{Regularized Primitive Dictionary}
A fundamental bottleneck in continuous optimization for SR is \textit{catastrophic cancellation}. When raw primitives (e.g., Lorentz $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1-u}$) approach the classical limit ($u \to 0$), the correction $\gamma - 1$ forces the numerical optimizer to resolve near-zero differences, destroying gradient signals.

We solve this through explicit algebraic regularization. Every primitive $D(u)$ in our dictionary is analytically shifted such that $\lim_{u \to 0} D(u) = 0$.
For example, the naive Lorentz correction $(1 - u)^{-0.5} - 1$ is analytically rationalized into our dictionary form $D_{lor}$:
\begin{equation}
D_{lor}(u) = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1-u}}{\sqrt{1-u}}
\end{equation}
This seemingly simple algebraic transformation yields massive numerical stability. In float32 precision, evaluating the naive formulation at $u = 10^{-6}$ results in a relative error of $\approx 19\%$, and completely collapses to $0.0$ at $u = 10^{-9}$. In contrast, our rationalized $D_{lor}$ maintains full numerical precision down to $u = 10^{-12}$, completely eliminating the need for gradient-based optimizers to guess exact coefficient cancellations.

Our constrained registry consists of exactly five regularized primitives: $D_{lor}$, $D_{rat}$, $D_{exp}$, $D_{log}$, and $D_{sqrt\_inv}$.

\subsection{Deterministic Enumeration}
Instead of stochastic mutation, ADCD employs deterministic Extended Backus-Naur Form (EBNF) grammar enumeration. By restricting the search space to the five regularized primitives, a maximum depth of 3, and a strict token budget (max tokens $\le 25$), the system generates a finite, exactly reproducible set of candidate equations. We strictly disclose the search space size for every experiment; the permutations typically evaluate in the tens or low hundreds of candidates. This constrained architecture not only prevents oracle leakage but also renders the framework extraordinarily computationally efficient, requiring no GPU acceleration and evaluating complete validation protocols in seconds on standard consumer hardware.

\subsection{Physics Gates and Optimization}
Candidate equations are subjected to strict Asymptotic Regime Checks (ARC) and dimensional analysis before numerical fitting. The surviving equations are continuously optimized using JAX in float64 precision. The necessity of float64 is directly tied to overcoming residual catastrophic cancellation in highly non-linear physics domains. Finally, model selection is performed strictly via BIC, replacing arbitrary parsimony pressures.

\subsection{The Four-Step Validation Protocol}
To prevent false-positive structural claims, a scenario must pass four explicit checks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Budget Disclosure:} The exact combinatorial search space size must be reported.
    \item \textbf{Positive Control:} The system must isolate the correct structure when the search space is restricted to single primitives.
    \item \textbf{Ablation Control:} The identified structure must dominate the baseline (e.g., a Taylor polynomial) explicitly via BIC-diff, not just raw Mean Squared Error (NMSE).
    \item \textbf{Determinism Check:} The output must be byte-identical across multiple independent runs.
\end{enumerate}
This protocol was born from extensive adversarial debugging, which revealed that raw NMSE and uncontrolled parameter budgets frequently mask structural mismatch.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Identifiability Sweep}
Our primary claim is ADCD's ability to map the boundary of structural identifiability. We swept the Time Dilation scenario across increasing data ranges (maximum velocity $v/c$). At $v/c = 0.80$, the BIC difference between the true Lorentz structure and a generic polynomial is a mere $+42.7$ (barely distinguishable from noise). As the regime extends to $v/c = 0.99$, the BIC-diff monotonically explodes to $+2075.6$. 
This behavior is independently predicted by the shape-correlation $R^2$ of the primitives, which drops from $0.990$ to $0.853$ in the same interval. ADCD explicitly utilizes this threshold to state when a dataset is mathematically insufficient to support a structural discovery, effectively reporting ``not identifiable.''

\subsection{Paradigm Comparison}
We do not claim ADCD achieves higher raw accuracy than PySR in general. Instead, we demonstrate that unconstrained SR structural recovery is highly sensitive to hyperparameters not exposed as scientific claims in typical reporting. In our tests on identical data, shifting PySR's parsimony parameter caused structural recovery success to whiplash from 0/10 to 9/10. ADCD's deterministic enumeration and BIC selection produces hyperparameter-stable results, and critically, reports explicitly when the underlying data cannot support a confident structural claim.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
ADCD is constrained by several conscious design decisions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Perturbative Blindness:} The ARC gate structurally demands that $\lim_{u \to 0} \Delta = 0$. Consequently, the system is fundamentally blind to non-perturbative phenomena where classical physics breaks down discontinuously.
    \item \textbf{Restricted Registry:} The choice of 5 primitives is a deliberate trade-off prioritizing tractability and identifiability over universal coverage. Generalization to physics anomalies requiring structures outside this registry remains untested.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Thresholds:} The AST constraints (depth $\le 7$, tokens $\le 25$) were derived as empirical engineering constants to limit combinatorial explosion, not from first-principles physics.
    \item \textbf{Principled Extensibility (Future Work):} The current registry of five primitives is a deliberate choice for tractable exhaustive enumeration and auditable identifiability analysis. Extending the registry is architecturally straightforward, but not trivial to validate: each additional primitive requires independent proof of algebraic regularization, numerical precision auditing, and identifiability re-analysis against every existing primitive pair---a burden that grows combinatorially. Beyond a modest registry size, exhaustive deterministic enumeration becomes computationally intractable, forcing a return to stochastic sampling and forfeiting the reproducibility guarantee central to this framework. We leave principled extensibility---including mandatory per-primitive validation gates and hard separation between validated `core' results and unvalidated `extended' results---as future work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
By regularizing primitive forms into $D(u) - D(0)$, ADCD removes numerical optimization bottlenecks inherent to asymptotic limits. By substituting unconstrained stochastic search with deterministic grammar enumeration and BIC validation, the framework provides an explicit identifiability boundary, actively refusing to ``discover'' structures when observational data is insufficient. Ultimately, ADCD is a verification-and-reconstruction instrument for a well-defined class of correction-discovery problems, not a general-purpose replacement for symbolic regression.
