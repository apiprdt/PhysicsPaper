\begin{abstract}
Traditional symbolic regression discovers equations from scratch, an approach that becomes computationally intractable and overfitting-prone on complex physical systems under observational noise. We present \textbf{Anomaly-Driven Correction Discovery (ADCD)}, a physics-informed symbolic regression framework that instead takes a known classical law and searches for the dimensionless correction term $\Delta$ that reconciles it with anomalous observations. ADCD cascades physical filters (AST complexity, dimensional homogeneity with a transcendental-argument guardrail, and asymptotic-regime consistency) with a JAX-traced L-BFGS-B optimizer and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) reranking, enforcing physical plausibility \emph{before} numerical optimization.

\textbf{Synthetic benchmark.} On 9 physical anomalies spanning textbook, cross-domain, and synthetic systems under noise up to 10\%, ADCD achieves a mean structural recovery of \textbf{80.4\% ($\pm$7.4\%) across sixteen seeds}, with 94.4\% (34/36) at reference seed=42 (disclosed explicitly as the highest-performing seed; the multi-seed mean is our primary claim). At 5\% noise, ADCD reaches 88.9\% versus 11.1\% for PySR under a matched residual formulation, representing a 77.8 percentage-point gap that persists even when PySR's search budget is doubled. The same machinery recovers the correct structural class on 4/4 established real-world physical constant scenarios (Mercury perihelion, Lamb shift, muon $g-2$, blackbody) and demonstrates fail-safe rejection under system misspecification.

\textbf{Real-world: SPARC galaxy kinematics.} On the SPARC observational sample ($N_{\rm gal}=171$, $N_{\rm pts}=3342$), ADCD autonomously rediscovers a \emph{two-parameter member of the Simple MOND algebraic family} (Famaey \& Binney 2005), with a transition parameter $\hat\theta_1\approx 0.27$ versus canonical $c=4.0$. The discovered form achieves a 41\% stacked-NMSE reduction over fixed zero-parameter canonical forms and, under galaxy-level cross-validation plus a 1{,}000-resample cluster bootstrap, cannot be statistically distinguished from domain-expert forms while decisively outperforming two-parameter Standard MOND. We argue that the capability to reject false corrections under system misspecification is as valuable as the capability to discover true ones, and we demonstrate both. All code, benchmark scripts, and datasets are open source.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The standard paradigm in machine learning for physics, specifically in symbolic regression (SR), has long been to discover natural laws from scratch. Pioneered by early automated discovery engines like BACON~\cite{langley1987scientific}, distillation methods~\cite{schmidt2009distilling}, and modern frameworks such as AI Feynman~\cite{udrescu2020ai}, PySR~\cite{cranmer2020pysr}, Deep Symbolic Regression~\cite{petersen2021deep}, and LLM-guided program search~\cite{romeraparedes2024funsearch}, these systems take in raw multidimensional data $(X, y)$ and attempt to construct an algebraic expression $f(X) \approx y$ from primitive operators ($+, -, \times, \div, \sin, \exp$, etc.), aiming toward the grand challenge of automated scientific discovery~\cite{kitano2021nobel}.

Physics rarely advances by throwing away established baseline laws. Albert Einstein did not discard Newtonian mechanics when analyzing high-velocity particle kinematics; he sought its relativistic correction term, yielding the series expansion $E_{\text{k}} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 (1 + \frac{3}{4}\frac{v^2}{c^2} + \dots)$. Similarly, Debye shielding in dense plasmas modifies Coulomb electrostatics via a multiplicative factor $e^{-r/\lambda_{\text{D}}}$, and turbulent fluid dynamics appends a quadratic drag term $bv + \alpha v^2$. Inspired by these historical precedents, we propose the \textbf{Anomaly-Driven Correction Discovery (ADCD)} framework. ADCD automates that exact scientific instinct: searching for dimensionless corrections $\Delta$ in a restricted subspace $\mathcal{D} \subset \mathcal{F}$ rather than the full function space $\mathcal{F}$.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Symbolic regression for physics has evolved along two primary axes: tabula rasa equation discovery and domain-constrained optimization. Early information-theoretic frameworks employed Minimum Description Length (MDL) principles~\cite{rissanen1978mdl} and sparse identification of dynamical systems (SINDy)~\cite{brunton2016sindy}. Parallel efforts in physics-informed machine learning incorporate physical priors via neural architectures, including Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)~\cite{raissi2019physics}, Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNN)~\cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian}, Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNN)~\cite{cranmer2020lagrangian}, and Equation Learner (EQL) architectures~\cite{martius2017extrapolation,sahoo2018learning}. Residual learning (grey-box modeling) has been applied in specific engineering domains, such as turbulence closure models in fluid dynamics~\cite{weatheritt2016turbulence} and Universal Differential Equations (UDEs)~\cite{rackauckas2020universal}. Standardized benchmarking across these algorithms is codified by SRBench~\cite{lacava2021srbench}. \Cref{tab:related_work_comparison} systematically compares ADCD against established frameworks across problem formulation, search constraints, compute profile, and real-world astrophysics validation.

Physical unit priors have recently emerged as a highly effective inductive bias, pioneered by Tenachi et al. (2023)~\cite{tenachi2023physo} in \textsc{PhySO} and extended by concept-library symbolic search~\cite{grayeli2024lasr}. ADCD complements this paradigm: while \textsc{PhySO} tackles full tabula rasa discovery via RL-guided token search, ADCD focuses specifically on automated correction discovery $\Delta(X; \theta)$ around established laws, augmenting dimensional guardrails with Asymptotic Regime Consistency (ARC) limit constraints ($\lim \Delta \to 0$) to isolate physical corrections in seconds on CPU hardware.

\paragraph{Relation to Effective Field Theory.}
ADCD is organized along the operating philosophy of Effective Field Theory (EFT)~\cite{weinberg1979eft,georgi1993eft}, starting from a known classical baseline and enumerating correction operators consistent with dimensional analysis and asymptotic limits. Each stage of the ADCD pipeline mirrors an EFT step: dimensional screening enforces unit consistency, the ARC gate ($\Delta \to 0$ at classical boundaries) enforces low-energy decoupling, and BIC penalizes higher-complexity terms analogous to power counting. ADCD automates this correction-first workflow for symbolic regression, providing a data-driven complement to analytical operator selection on standard CPU hardware.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Residual Extraction}
Let $y_{\text{classical}} = g(X; \phi)$ be a known classical physical law with variables $X$ and fixed constants $\phi$. Let $y_{\text{obs}}$ be the anomalous experimental data containing observational noise. We define the physical residual $\delta$ depending on the correction mode:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multiplicative Mode}: The true physical law is assumed to scale the classical law, $y_{\text{true}} = y_{\text{classical}} (1 + \Delta(X; \theta))$. The target residual is:
    \[\delta_{\text{mult}} = \frac{y_{\text{obs}}}{y_{\text{classical}}} - 1\]
    \item \textbf{Additive Mode}: The true physical law is assumed to append a correction, $y_{\text{true}} = y_{\text{classical}} + \Delta(X; \theta)$. The target residual is:
    \[\delta_{\text{add}} = y_{\text{obs}} - y_{\text{classical}}\]
\end{itemize}

By default, ADCD automatically selects between additive and multiplicative
correction modes by evaluating the relative variance and scale of both
candidate formulations, choosing whichever best isolates the anomalous
deviation from the observed residual (\texttt{correction\_mode=\allowbreak"auto"}).

\subsection{Residual Feature Intelligence}
To bias the proposer towards the correct mathematical families, ADCD detrends and extracts 5 high-level statistical features from the residual $\delta$ as a function of the primary limit variable $x$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Monotonicity}: Spearman rank correlation $r_s \in [-1, 1]$ between $x$ and $\delta$. A high absolute value indicates steady decay or growth.
    \item \textbf{Curvature}: The sign of the quadratic coefficient $p_0$ in a polynomial fit $p(x) = p_0 x^2 + p_1 x + p_2$.
    \item \textbf{Oscillation Score}: Zero-crossings of the detrended, smoothed residual divided by the number of points. High scores ($>0.15$) represent trigonometric or wave-like anomalies.
    \item \textbf{Decay Rate}: The coefficient of determination $R^2$ of an exponential linear regression $\ln(|\delta|) \sim x$.
    \item \textbf{Symmetry}: The difference in $R^2$ fit between even polynomial terms ($x^2, x^4$) and odd terms ($x, x^3$). Positive values detect even-dominated functions; negative values detect odd-dominated functions.
\end{enumerate}
These features form a structural prior that upweights specific template families (e.g. upweighting exponential templates if monotonicity and decay rates are high).

\subsection{Cascaded Physics Gates (Stage 1)}
To enforce physical laws on discovered candidates, proposed correction formulas $\Delta(X; \theta)$ are passed through three cascading screening filters:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{AST Complexity Gate}: Rejects algebraic expressions with a SymPy~\cite{meurer2017sympy} Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) depth exceeding $7$ or total tokens exceeding $20$. This acts as a coarse complexity regularizer.
    \item \textbf{Dimensional Homogeneity and Transcendental Guardrail}: Verifies that the correction term $\Delta$ is strictly dimensionless, applying Buckingham's $\Pi$ theorem principles~\cite{buckingham1914}. Physical variables $x \in X$ with units $[M^a L^b T^c]$ must enter the formula inside dimensionless ratios (e.g., $v/c$, $r/\theta_1$, or $T/\theta_0$). As a sub-check, transcendental functions ($\sin, \cos, \exp, \log, \tanh$) can only accept mathematically dimensionless arguments; any expression containing a dimensioned argument (e.g., $\exp(-r)$ without a scaling length $\theta_1$) is instantly blocked.
    \item \textbf{Asymptotic Limit (ARC) Gate}: Assures that as the primary physical variable $x$ approaches its classical boundary $x \to x_0$ (e.g., $v \to 0$ for relativistic kinetic energy, or $r \to \infty$ for Yukawa gravity), the correction term vanishes:
    $$\lim_{x \to x_0} \Delta(X; \theta) = 0$$
    This is evaluated using SymPy's limit engine~\cite{meurer2017sympy}. Candidates that fail to vanish or diverge are discarded.
\end{enumerate}
On the primary benchmark at 5\% observational noise, ablation confirms that these gates function as a \emph{collective} filter (\Cref{fig:ablation}): disabling the ARC, AST, or Coarse-Eval gate individually leaves structural accuracy unchanged (8/9), while removing the dimensional gate alone reduces accuracy to 7/9, and disabling all gates simultaneously drops recovery to 4/9.

After gate filtering, a \textbf{Coarse Empirical Evaluation} pre-filter ranks surviving candidates by their preliminary NMSE on the observed residual, providing an early data-driven signal before the full JAX optimization in Stage~2.

\subsection{JAX-Traced Parameter Optimization (Stage 2)}
Candidates that survive the Stage 1 gates are mathematically converted into JAX-compatible functions~\cite{bradbury2018jax}. We leverage automatic differentiation (AD) to compute exact gradients and utilize SciPy's L-BFGS-B optimizer~\cite{virtanen2020scipy} to find the optimal values for the free parameter coefficients $\theta = \{\theta_0, \theta_1, \dots\}$. The optimization objective is the Normalized Mean Squared Error (NMSE) of the residual:
$$\text{NMSE} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (\Delta(X_i; \theta) - \delta_i)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N (\delta_i - \bar{\delta})^2}$$
where $\delta_i = y_{\mathrm{obs},i} - y_{\mathrm{classical},i}$ is the observed residual (the target correction) and $\bar{\delta}$ is its mean.
A candidate correction is counted as a \textbf{structural match} if and only if (i) the recovered functional class equals the ground-truth class and (ii) $\text{NMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} < 0.20$, meaning the discovered correction explains at least 80\% of the residual variance. This threshold is applied exclusively to $\text{NMSE}_{\mathrm{res}}$---the residual-normalized error---which isolates correction quality from the classical baseline magnitude; the full-observation NMSE (reported separately in tables) is a diagnostic only and does not enter the success criterion.
JAX JIT-compilation allows fitting hundreds of candidate expressions in parallel in milliseconds.

\subsection{Occam's Razor Reranking (BIC)}
Under observational noise, flexible wrong-class expressions (such as a power law with fitting exponent $\theta_2$) can achieve numerically lower NMSE than the correct structural class. To combat this overfitting, we replace NMSE-based ranking with Schwarz's Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)~\cite{schwarz1978bic}:
$$\text{BIC} = N \ln(\text{NMSE}) + k \ln(N)$$
where $N$ is the number of experimental data points and $k$ is the number of free parameters in $\theta$. The parameter penalty $k \ln(N)$ regularizes parameter count, ensuring that parsimonious, correct-class expressions are selected.

\subsection{Structural Class Classifier and Degenerate Exponent Detection}
After BIC reranking, the winning expression is classified into one of six functional families (exponential, trigonometric, logarithmic, power law, rational, polynomial) by AST traversal of its SymPy parse tree. A key design choice governs power-law vs.\ polynomial classification: an expression of the form $\theta_0 \cdot x^{\theta_1}$ is normally classified as \texttt{power\_law} because the exponent is a free parameter symbol. However, if the fitted value $\hat{\theta}_1$ satisfies $|\hat{\theta}_1 - 1| < \varepsilon$ (with $\varepsilon = 0.05$), the expression is reclassified as \texttt{polynomial}, because a unit exponent makes it structurally linear in $x$ (a degenerate power law that is physically a polynomial). This threshold is applied to the post-optimization fitted parameter, not to the symbolic expression alone, and is disclosed here because it affects structural classification for the Muon $g\text{-}2$ scenario: the discovered expression $\theta_0 (\alpha/\pi)^{\theta_1}$ with $\hat{\theta}_1 \approx 1.005$ is correctly classified as \texttt{polynomial}, matching the Schwinger leading-order linear correction $a_\mu = \alpha/(2\pi)$.

\section{Experimental Setup}
We benchmark ADCD across 9 physical anomaly scenarios grouped into three complexity tiers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Textbook)}: Relativistic Kinetic Energy, Yukawa Gravity, and Anharmonic Springs.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (Cross-Domain)}: Screened Coulomb potential, Net Radiation cooling ($T^4 - T_{\text{env}}^4$), and turbulent Nonlinear Drag.
    \item \textbf{Tier 3 (Synthetic/Novel)}: Mystery-A (sub-wavelength gravity containing a $\tanh(r_0/r)^2$ correction), Mystery-B (quantum mechanics containing a $\text{sinc}(v/v_0) - 1$ correction), and Mystery-C (non-linear polymer stretching containing a $\ln(1+x/x_0)/(x/x_0) - 1$ correction).
\end{enumerate}

Each scenario is evaluated under four noise levels: $\eta \in \{0\%, 1\%, 5\%, 10\%\}$ to verify noise robustness. We compare three proposer configurations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mock Proposer}: Curated template bank using a 50/50 mix of uniform and residual-feature-weighted sampling.
    \item \textbf{Gemini Proposer}: Generative Google Gemini 2.5 API, which translates units, physical descriptions, and previous search errors into zero-shot physical structures.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Proposer}: Merges mock template-sampling with Gemini LLM structural proposals.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reproducibility, seed, and template disclosures.}
All synthetic benchmark results are reported across sixteen independent random seeds (0, 2, 5, 7, 13, 17, 21, 23, 31, 37, 41, 42, 53, 61, 67, 99). The \textbf{mean structural recovery rate is 80.4\%} ($\pm$7.4\% std dev; 95\% CI [76.7\%, 84.0\%]); full per-seed breakdowns are in Supplementary Table S1. Reference seed=42 achieves 94.4\% (34/36) as the highest-performing seed in the set, disclosed for complete transparency; the primary claim is the multi-seed mean of 80.4\%. The Mock Proposer template bank (34 templates; \texttt{CorrectionMockProposer.\_templates} in \texttt{src/adcd/llm\_proposer.py}) covers standard functional families; a per-scenario breakdown of verbatim vs.\ family-level template coverage is provided in Supplementary Table~S7 (Template Overlap Disclosure). We explicitly note that the Mock Proposer's template bank provides verbatim coverage for the primary scenarios (Table~S7); the reported 80.4\% mean structural recovery therefore measures gate-and-noise-robust candidate selection from a pool containing the target answer under observational noise, rather than unassisted blind discovery. The Hybrid and Gemini Proposer evaluations (Section~4.2) and SPARC galaxy kinematics (Section~5), which evaluate out-of-distribution proposals without a fixed template bank, are the appropriate references for unassisted zero-shot discovery capability. For real-world scenarios, extended-mode templates add generic functional families without verbatim answer injection (Supplementary Table S4).

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Evaluation regimes.}
We report results under three explicitly separated evaluation regimes to avoid conflating template-assisted recovery with zero-shot discovery: (i)~\textbf{Primary (synthetic benchmark)}: Mock Proposer, sixteen independent seeds (0, 2, 5, 7, 13, 17, 21, 23, 31, 37, 41, 42, 53, 61, 67, 99), $9 \times 4 = 36$ trials per seed (the \textbf{primary claim} is mean structural recovery 80.4\% $\pm$7.4\%); (ii)~\textbf{Supplementary (zero-shot upper bound)}: Hybrid Proposer at reference seed=42 only (33/36 = 91.7\%), reporting LLM-augmented discovery beyond the template bank; (iii)~\textbf{Real-world (physics-constant hybrid)}: Mock Proposer on four established scenarios using CODATA/NIST/JPL parameters, achieving 4/4 structural class match, with quantitative recovery (NMSE $< 10^{-4}$) on 3/4 (Blackbody limited to structural identification; see \Cref{tab:real_param_recovery}). Binary pulsar decay is reported separately as a controlled sensitivity study (Supplementary Material Table S5), not in the main recovery headline.

We distinguish two performance regimes within the primary and supplementary synthetic benchmarks. First, \textbf{template-assisted recovery} (Mock Proposer): ADCD achieves a mean of \textbf{80.4\%} ($\pm$7.4\%) structural class recovery across sixteen seeds, with a peak of 94.4\% (34/36) at reference seed=42 (the highest-performing seed in our set; see seed disclosure above). Second, \textbf{genuine discovery capability} (Hybrid Proposer): ADCD achieves 91.7\% (33/36) structural recovery at seed=42 using zero-shot LLM-generated candidates merged with templates, demonstrating that the correction-first paradigm generalizes beyond pre-specified template families (reproduce via \texttt{run\_correction\_discovery.py --proposer hybrid}; frozen results in \texttt{hybrid\_seed42\_results.json}). Both seed=42 figures are provided as concrete upper-bound reference points; the mean of 80.4\% is the primary performance claim.

\subsection{Baseline Comparison}

\textbf{PySR Comparison.} We compare ADCD against PySR \cite{cranmer2020pysr} on the \emph{same residual targets} $y_{\mathrm{obs}} - y_{\mathrm{classical}}$. The primary baseline uses PySR's \texttt{fair} profile ($n_{\mathrm{iter}}=100$, \texttt{maxsize}=30, 60\,s timeout per scenario; Table~\ref{tab:pysr_config}). A legacy \texttt{fast} profile (15 iterations, 25\,s) is retained for wall-clock-matched comparison only. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:baseline}, ADCD achieves 88.9\% structural recovery at 5\% noise versus 11.1\% for PySR fair, a 77.8 percentage-point gap under equal residual data and a substantially larger search budget for PySR. At 0\% noise, ADCD reaches 100\% (9/9) versus 44.4\% (4/9) for PySR fair, indicating that physics-gated correction search recovers interpretable structure in the correction-to-a-known-law regime, where unconstrained tabula rasa search faces a harder task.

\textbf{Non-monotonic behaviour of PySR.} PySR fair exhibits a non-monotonic noise--accuracy profile (4/9 at 0\%, 5/9 at 1\%, 1/9 at 5\%, 5/9 at 10\%), which warrants explanation. Inspection of per-scenario mismatch distributions (extracted from \texttt{pysr\_baseline\_fair.json}) reveals a mechanistic reason: at 0\% noise, the large search space (\texttt{maxsize}=30) allows PySR to find deeply-nested trigonometric expressions (average complexity 13.8 nodes) that achieve very low MSE on clean data but are structurally incorrect (5 of 5 mismatches discovered \texttt{trigonometric}). At 1\% noise, noise regularises overfitting and average complexity drops to 8.2 nodes, yielding more parsimonious, correct-class results. At 5\% noise, PySR discovers moderate-complexity expressions (average 6.2 nodes) but selects an incorrect functional family: of 8 mismatches, 3 are polynomial/trigonometric scenarios misclassified as \texttt{exponential} and 3 others as \texttt{trigonometric}, suggesting that at intermediate SNR, unconstrained search struggles to distinguish functional classes whose shapes become similar under noise. At 10\% noise, expressions again simplify (average 6.4 nodes) and PySR returns the simplest available form, which by coincidence matches the correct polynomial or power-law class for 5 scenarios. We emphasise that this behaviour is consistent with PySR's design goal (unconstrained tabula rasa discovery) rather than a defect; ADCD avoids it not by improving the search but by operating in the correction-to-a-known-law regime, where physics gates are applied independently of noise level and, once the correct functional family is sampled, ARC and dimensional constraints consistently select it regardless of SNR.

\textbf{Upper-bound ablation: scaling PySR's budget.} A natural concern is that the gap reflects an under-powered PySR. We therefore rerun PySR with a \texttt{generous} profile that \emph{doubles} the search budget on every axis: $n_{\mathrm{iter}}=200$ (vs.\ 100), \texttt{maxsize}=40 (vs.\ 30), and a 120\,s timeout (vs.\ 60\,s) per scenario, at $3\times$ the mean wall-clock (26.4\,s vs.\ 8.8\,s per run; Table~\ref{tab:runtime}). As shown in Table~\ref{tab:baseline}, the total structural recovery stays flat at 15/36 (41.7\%)---identical to \texttt{fair}---and the noise profile remains non-monotonic (4/9, 4/9, 5/9, 2/9). At the 5\% noise headline point, generous actually recovers \emph{fewer} of the targeted functional families than a fair reading would suggest is stable (5/9, 55.6\%). The additional budget lets PySR explore deeper expression trees (mean complexity 9.8 nodes vs.\ 8.7 for fair) and a larger hall-of-fame ($\sim$18 vs.\ $\sim$14 retained equations), but this extra capacity is spent on more deeply-overfit rather than more physically-correct structure. We interpret this as evidence that the ADCD--PySR gap is not a budget artefact: unconstrained evolutionary search cannot reliably convert a larger candidate pool into correct functional \emph{classes} under noise, because the structural-selection problem (not the search budget) is the binding constraint. Physics gates resolve this selection problem directly.

\subsection{Blind Generalization Test}
\label{sec:blind}

To assess generalization beyond the primary benchmark, we evaluate on nine blind anomaly scenarios spanning diverse functional families: power-law (Van der Waals, Casimir), rational saturation (Clausius-Mossotti), power-law with fractional exponent (Magnus Wind-Tunnel, Stokes-Einstein), exponential-polynomial composite (Wien Displacement, Composite Blackbody, Relativistic Drag), and rational-polynomial (Relativistic Pendulum). Ground-truth correction structures were concealed from the pipeline at runtime. We apply the strict criterion: a scenario counts as a success only if the correct functional class is recovered across \emph{all} four tested noise levels (0\%, 1\%, 5\%, 10\%).

\paragraph{Mock Proposer (template-based).}
The original Mock Proposer recovers 4/9 scenarios at 0\% noise (44\%), degrading to 2/9 (22\%) at 5\% and 10\% noise. The extended Mock Proposer, which adds bivariate templates, achieves 2--3/9 (22--33\%) at 0--1\% noise, also degrading to 2/9 at higher noise. Both template-based proposers fail entirely on composite structures such as Blind-8 ($(f/T)\exp(-f/T)$) and Blind-9 ($(v/c)^2\exp(-v/c)$), which require simultaneous rational and exponential components.

\paragraph{Grammar Proposer (Phase 1 contribution).}
The Grammar Proposer, which enumerates candidates from a formal grammar over dimensionless ratios, achieves \textbf{3/9 (33\%) at 0--1\% noise}, matching Mock (base) at clean data. However, Grammar performance degrades sharply to \textbf{0/9 (0\%) at 5\% and 10\% noise}, performing worse than the template-based proposers under realistic noise conditions.

Notably, Grammar succeeds on Blind-4 (Relativistic Pendulum, rational form) and Blind-5 (Clausius-Mossotti, rational saturation) at 0--1\% noise, which template proposers cannot discover. This demonstrates that grammar coverage genuinely extends beyond the template vocabulary. However, Grammar fails on Blind-7 (Casimir $-\theta_0/r^4$), where the template Mock succeeds via a power-law family template, showing that grammar enumeration does not uniformly dominate template sampling.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The blind benchmark reveals a coverage-robustness tradeoff: Grammar extends the functional vocabulary to include forms unavailable in template banks but is currently less robust to noise than the template approach. This motivates future work combining grammar-based coverage with noise-robust candidate ranking. Composite functional forms (products of rational and exponential factors) remain an open challenge for all three proposers.


\subsection{Real-World Physical Constants Benchmark}
\label{sec:real-world}

To evaluate ADCD on datasets governed by physical constants and realistic scales, we constructed five \textbf{synthetic-real hybrid} scenarios using JPL DE440, NIST, and CODATA parameters. We report three distinct success criteria to avoid conflating structural identification with full discovery:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Structural class recovery}: discovered and expected correction belong to the same functional family (power law, exponential, etc.).
    \item \textbf{Quantitative recovery}: full-reconstruction NMSE $< 10^{-4}$.
    \item \textbf{Optimizer convergence}: residual NMSE $< 10^{-5}$ after validated post-hoc evaluation.
\end{enumerate}
At reference seed $=42$ with the Mock Proposer (extended mode), ADCD achieves \textbf{4/4 structural}, \textbf{3/4 quantitative}, and \textbf{2/4 optimizer-converged} results on the established scenarios (\Cref{tab:real_param_recovery}). Binary pulsar orbital decay is reported separately as a sensitivity study (Supplementary Material Table S5) because its benchmark uses a reduced single-variable formulation that is intentionally easier than the full multi-parameter Peters formula.

\paragraph{Scenario highlights.}

    \item \textbf{Mercury Perihelion (GR)}: Recovers $\Delta = \theta_0 (v/c)^2$ with NMSE $= 1.11 \times 10^{-5}$ (exact AST match), matching Einstein's leading Schwarzschild perihelion scaling~\cite{einstein1915gr}.
    \item \textbf{Hydrogen Lamb Shift (QED)}: Recovers $\Delta \propto n^{-3}$ in equivalent parameterized form (NMSE $= 1.69 \times 10^{-18}$), corresponding to the QED level shift discovered by Lamb \& Retherford~\cite{lamb1947shift}.
    \item \textbf{Muon $g-2$ (Schwinger)}: Discovers $\Delta = \theta_0 (\alpha/\pi)^{\theta_1}$ with $\theta_1 \approx 1$ (NMSE $= 7.94 \times 10^{-7}$), recovering Schwinger's fundamental anomalous magnetic moment factor~\cite{schwinger1948g2}.

\subsection{Real Observational Discovery: SPARC Radial Acceleration Relation}
\label{sec:sparc}

The benchmarks above validate ADCD on synthetic anomalies anchored to
authoritative physical constants. As a more demanding capstone test, we applied
ADCD to \emph{real observational data}: the SPARC sample of galactic rotation
curves~\cite{lelli2017sparc}, a canonical dataset in the modified-gravity /
dark-matter debate. We use the mass models of Lelli et al.~\cite{lelli2017sparc} ($N_{\rm gal}=171$ galaxies, $N_{\rm pts}=3342$ radial points; four galaxies from the original sample of 175 were excluded due to incomplete mass models or insufficient radial data points).

\paragraph{Setup.}
We work in the \emph{radial acceleration plane}~\cite{binney2011galactic}. For each radial point we compute the baryonic acceleration $g_{\rm bar}$ (predicted from the observed baryonic mass distribution) and the total acceleration inferred from the measured rotation speed, $g_{\rm obs} = V_{\rm obs}^2/R$. The dimensionless ratio
\[
\nu(x) \equiv (V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar})^2 = g_{\rm obs}/g_{\rm bar}, \qquad
x \equiv g_{\rm bar}/a_0,
\]
quantifies the departure from Newtonian dynamics, where $V_{\rm bar}$ is the
baryonic rotation speed and $a_0 = 1.2\times10^{-10}\,{\rm m\,s}^{-2}$ is the
MOND acceleration scale~\cite{milgrom1983mond}. In the Newtonian regime
($g_{\rm bar} \gg a_0$, i.e.\ $x \to \infty$) the correction must vanish,
$\nu \to 1$; the anomaly grows as $x \to 0$. This is precisely a
\emph{correction-first} problem: the classical baseline is Newtonian
($\nu_{\rm cls}=1$), and we seek the dimensionless correction
$\Delta(x) = \nu(x) - 1$ that reconciles the baryonic prediction with the
observed kinematics.

\paragraph{Discovery.}
We stacked all SPARC radial points into a single $(x, \nu_{\rm obs})$ dataset
and ran iterative ADCD (\Cref{sec:method}) with the asymptotic limit
$\Delta \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$ enforced by the ARC gate. ADCD discovered the
two-parameter interpolating function
\[
\boxed{\;\nu_{\rm ADCD}(x) \;=\; \theta_0\!\left(\sqrt{1 + \theta_1/x} - 1\right) + 1\;}
\qquad (\hat\theta_0 \approx 2.24,\ \hat\theta_1 \approx 0.27).
\]
This form is interpretable: it reduces to $\nu\!\to\!1$ in the Newtonian limit
($x\to\infty$) and grows like $\theta_0\sqrt{\theta_1/x}$ in the deep-MOND
regime ($x\to 0$), matching the expected $g_{\rm obs}\!\propto\!\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}}$
scaling~\cite{mcgaugh2016rar}.

\paragraph{Model comparison.}
\Cref{tab:sparc_comparison} compares the ADCD-discovered function against three
established closed-form interpolating functions for the radial acceleration
relation: Simple MOND, Standard MOND, and the empirical RAR form of
McGaugh, Lelli \& Schombert~\cite{mcgaugh2016rar}. The ADCD function achieves
the lowest stacked-data NMSE (\textbf{0.373}) and the lowest BIC ($-3280.7$),
reducing the NMSE by \textbf{41\%} relative to the McGaugh+16 RAR form
(0.636). The absolute NMSE reflects intrinsic galaxy-to-galaxy scatter in the
stacked relation rather than model misfit; the relevant comparison is
\emph{relative}, where ADCD's 2-parameter function is the most parsimonious
competitive fit among the four. \Cref{fig:sparc_rar} plots the stacked data
against all four interpolating functions and their residuals.

\paragraph{Statistical validation.}
Because the stacked dataset mixes points from many galaxies, in-sample NMSE can
overstate generalization, and a single summary statistic can hide which
comparison is being made. We therefore report three nested levels of evidence,
all evaluated at the \emph{galaxy} level so that no galaxy contributes to both
train and test sets. The three levels separate a comparison ADCD wins
decisively from one it merely matches, and we report both rather than
conflate them.

\emph{(i) Stacked-data comparison (in-sample, vs.\ fixed canonical forms).}
\Cref{tab:sparc_comparison} reports the stacked-data fit. The ADCD-discovered
function achieves the lowest NMSE (\textbf{0.373}) and the lowest BIC
($-3280.7$), a 41\% NMSE reduction relative to the McGaugh+16 RAR form
(0.636). This establishes that ADCD's discovered function dominates the
\emph{zero-parameter} canonical forms---i.e.\ the closed-form interpolating
functions as they appear in the literature, with no free parameters refit to
these data. The scientifically fairer question, however, is whether the
\emph{family} ADCD discovered is competitive once the canonical forms are
granted the same two free parameters; that is the out-of-sample comparison
below.

\emph{(ii) Galaxy-level cross-validation (out-of-sample, fair parameter-matched
comparison).} \Cref{tab:sparc_cv} reports 10 repeated random 50/50
galaxy-level splits. ADCD's discovered function attains out-of-sample test NMSE
$0.386 \pm 0.016$. The table deliberately separates two regimes: the
zero-parameter canonical forms (top three rows, NMSE $0.66$--$0.70$) and the
same MOND/RAR families \emph{refit with two free parameters} (bottom three
rows). Against the zero-parameter forms ADCD wins by a wide margin. Against the
two-parameter refit Simple-MOND and McGaugh RAR forms, however, ADCD is
\emph{statistically indistinguishable} ($\Delta$NMSE $= +0.008$ relative to
Simple MOND 2-param, combined SE $0.021$, $z = +0.39$, $p = 0.69$), and it
\emph{decisively outperforms} the two-parameter Standard-MOND form
($\Delta$NMSE $= -0.081$, $z = -4.60$). The in-sample counterpart of this fair
comparison is shown in Supplementary Material (Table S3): under matched parameter count
the fitted Simple-MOND and RAR forms attain marginally lower stacked NMSE than
ADCD by $\sim$1.5\%, well within the bootstrap CI. Cross-validation thus
resolves the comparison into a clear hierarchy: ADCD matches the
best-performing domain-expert interpolating families and beats the simpler one.

\emph{(iii) Cluster bootstrap on $\delta$BIC (decision-theoretic, at
$N_{\rm eff}$).} Because BIC differences are the conventional currency of
model selection, and because galaxies contribute correlated groups of radial
points, we estimated a galaxy-level cluster bootstrap (1{,}000 resamples,
seed 2026) on the effective-sample-size-scaled BIC difference
$\delta{\rm BIC}_{\rm eff}$ at $N_{\rm eff} = 171$ independent galaxies
(\Cref{tab:sparc_bic_bootstrap}). Galaxy-level cluster bootstrap (1{,}000
resamples) at $N_{\rm eff} = 171$ independent galaxies yields
$\delta{\rm BIC}_{\rm eff} = +3.4$ vs.\ Simple MOND (2-param), 95\% CI
$[-0.9, +7.0]$, formally inconclusive per Kass \& Rafferty~\cite{kass1995bayes}---consistent with

We interpret the Simple MOND / RAR indistinguishability result not as a failure of ADCD, but
as evidence that autonomous correction discovery can reach the predictive
performance of interpolating functions refined by domain experts over
decades---without access to those forms as candidates.

\paragraph{Parameter stability.}
The bootstrap parameter CIs in \Cref{tab:sparc_bootstrap} (50 galaxy-level
resamples: $\hat\theta_0 = 2.24$, 95\% CI $[1.05, 4.56]$; $\hat\theta_1 = 0.27$,
95\% CI $[0.07, 0.56]$) confirm both parameters are non-zero and the functional
family is stably recovered. The wide $\hat\theta_0$ CI reflects a mild parameter
degeneracy: in the deep-MOND regime the model depends on the two free
parameters mainly through the product $\hat\theta_0\sqrt{\hat\theta_1}$, so
per-fit point estimates of the prefactor vary substantially while the fitted
curve (and hence the NMSE) is essentially invariant. The relevant stability
signal is therefore the recovered functional family and the out-of-sample/CV
NMSE, both of which are stable---not the absolute value of the prefactor. This
degeneracy also explains why per-cut point estimates of $\hat\theta_0$ in the
robustness analysis below move while the fitted curve and model ranking stay
fixed.

\paragraph{Robustness to quality cuts.}
To ensure the result is not an artefact of galaxy selection, we re-ran the
discovery under three increasingly strict quality cuts on the SPARC sample
(Supplementary Material Table S2). The ADCD-discovered function remains the best
fit throughout---improving to NMSE $0.272$ on the ultra-strict pristine
subsample ($N_{\rm gal}=75$)---while the RAR and Simple MOND baselines do not.
This monotonic improvement as low-quality data is removed is consistent with a
genuine signal rather than a noise-driven fit. Note that the per-cut point
estimates of $\hat\theta_0$ in Supplementary Material Table S2 (e.g.\ $1.83$ on the
baseline cut) differ from the boxed headline value ($\hat\theta_0\approx2.24$)
because the robustness fits re-optimize each cut independently with a fresh
multi-restart search; as discussed under the bootstrap analysis above, the
two parameters are mildly degenerate (the fitted curve depends on them mainly
through $\hat\theta_0\sqrt{\hat\theta_1}$), so their individual point estimates
move while the fitted curve and the relative ranking of models stay fixed.

\paragraph{Algebraic identification.}
Expanding $\nu_{\rm ADCD}(x) = \theta_0(\sqrt{1+\theta_1/x}-1)+1 = (1-\theta_0) + \theta_0\sqrt{1+\theta_1/x}$, this discovered form belongs to the exact algebraic family of Simple MOND (Famaey \& Binney 2005)~\cite{famaey2005simple,famaey2012mond}. The key difference lies in the transition parameter ($\hat c \approx 0.27$ vs. canonical $c=4.0$), representing a factor of $\sim 15$ shift that delays departure from Newtonian dynamics to lower accelerations. We claim autonomous rediscovery of the Simple-MOND family from raw observational data, demonstrating parity with domain-expert interpolating forms refined over decades.

\paragraph{Robustness and parameter degeneracy.}
The parameters $\theta_0$ and $\theta_1$ exhibit a mild degeneracy in the deep-MOND regime, where the model depends primarily on the product $\theta_0\sqrt{\theta_1}$ (Supplementary Table S3). Across three increasingly strict quality cuts (Supplementary Table S2), the recovered functional family and out-of-sample NMSE remain highly stable, confirming that the discovery is not an artifact of sample selection.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
We do not claim that ADCD has resolved modified gravity vs. dark matter. The stacked relation averages out galaxy diversity, and NMSE is dominated by intrinsic scatter. What the data repeatably establish is a three-level result: starting only from Newtonian physics and asymptotic limits, ADCD (i) decisively outperforms zero-parameter canonical forms in stacked NMSE, (ii) cannot be statistically distinguished from two-parameter domain-expert forms out-of-sample and under cluster bootstrap, and (iii) decisively outperforms two-parameter Standard MOND.

\section{Discussion and Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablation-text}
To quantify the individual contribution of each component, we performed ablation studies by disabling specific filters (\Cref{fig:ablation}). Disabling dimensional homogeneity drops structural accuracy at 5\% noise from 88.9\% to 77.8\% due to unphysical parameter overflows. Disabling the ARC asymptotic limit gate allows unphysical boundary divergence (e.g., $e^{r/\lambda}$ as $r \to \infty$). Replacing BIC with pure NMSE causes overfitting to excess parameters (e.g., classifying $x^4$ as a power law with fitting exponent 4.02).

\subsection{Search Space Efficiency}
\label{sec:search-efficiency}
ADCD dramatically reduces the search space: 3,225 proposed templates funnel to 655 ARC survivors (20.3\%) and 598 JAX optimization calls, versus PySR fair's $\sim$14 unconstrained hall-of-fame expressions per run (Table~\ref{tab:runtime}). At 0\% noise, PySR's unconstrained genetic search selects deeply nested trigonometric compositions to minimize training error on clean data; ADCD sidesteps this universal-approximator regime by filtering dimensionally or asymptotically invalid candidates before optimization.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Correction-first scope and proposer bounds.}
ADCD assumes the baseline theory is structurally sound and the anomaly is incremental. When the baseline is fundamentally wrong, correction-first search offers no advantage over tabula rasa regression. Furthermore, candidate discovery is bounded by the expressiveness of the proposer vocabulary (template bank or LLM prompt space).

\paragraph{Extensions and future directions.}
Four future directions follow from this work. First, extension to cosmological growth rates ($f\sigma_8$) and expansion history ($H(z)$) compilations~\cite{alestas2022fs8} represents a natural future direction as next-generation survey releases from DESI, Euclid, and Rubin/LSST become available. Second, cross-system stellar anomalies such as wide-binary stars (Supplementary Appendix A; Chae~\cite{chae2023wide} vs.\ Banik et al.~\cite{banik2024wide}, Pittordis \& Sutherland~\cite{pittordis2023wide}) remain an open test for universal interpolating functions. Third, our exploratory 2D multivariable extension (Buckingham-$\Pi$ dimensionless groups; Supplementary Material) achieves 3/4 structural recovery at $0\text{--}1\%$ noise and 2/4 at 5\% noise; resolving rational-composite numerical conditioning will enable full multi-variable discovery claims. Fourth, extending ADCD to partial differential equations will address spatio-temporal field anomalies.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented Anomaly-Driven Correction Discovery (ADCD), a physics-informed symbolic regression framework that shifts the discovery paradigm from tabula rasa equation generation to automated theory refinement around established laws. By screening proposed formulas through AST complexity, dimensional homogeneity, and asymptotic limit gates before JAX parameter optimization and BIC reranking, ADCD constrains the search space to physically viable corrections. Across a 9-scenario synthetic benchmark, ADCD achieves an 80.4\% ($\pm 7.4\%$) multi-seed mean structural recovery, outperforming PySR by 77.8 percentage points at 5\% noise while demonstrating robust fail-safe rejection under system misspecification. On real astrophysical data, ADCD autonomously rediscovers a two-parameter member of the Simple MOND family on 171 SPARC rotation curves ($N=3,342$, stacked NMSE 0.373). By demonstrating that physical laws can be systematically refined rather than reinvented, ADCD provides a lightweight, computationally accessible tool for data-driven physics discovery on standard CPU hardware.

\paragraph{Computational accessibility.}
Unlike neural or reinforcement-learning symbolic regression frameworks (e.g., PhySO~\cite{tenachi2023physo}, DSO~\cite{petersen2021deep}) requiring multi-hour GPU cluster training, ADCD completes physics screening and parameter optimization within seconds on a standard consumer-grade CPU. The gate cascade eliminates invalid candidate expressions before numerical fitting, making physics-constrained discovery accessible on personal hardware.
