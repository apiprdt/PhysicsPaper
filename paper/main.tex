\section{Introduction}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{How physics actually advances}

In 1687, Newton gave physics its first universal law of motion.
For two centuries, it worked.
Then, in 1887, the Michelson--Morley experiment demonstrated that the speed of
light is invariant regardless of the observer's frame~\cite{michelson1887}.
Einstein's resolution in 1905 was not to discard Newton~\cite{einstein1905}.
It was to multiply the proper time interval by a factor
$\gamma = (1 - v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, a correction that reduces to exactly 1
when $v \ll c$.
Newtonian mechanics remained valid at low speeds. The Lorentz factor told us
\emph{when} it needed to be extended.

This pattern -- extend, not replace -- recurs throughout physics.
Coulomb's electrostatic force is screened by an exponential factor
$e^{-r/\lambda_D}$ in plasmas, where free charges attenuate the field at the
Debye length $\lambda_D$~\cite{debye1923}.
The entropy of an ideal gas undergoing isothermal expansion receives a
logarithmic correction $\Delta S = nR\ln(V_f/V_i)$, a standard result of
classical thermodynamics~\cite{callen1985}.
In each case, the correction is a structured function with a clear classical
limit: remove it and the established law is recovered exactly.

\subsection{The problem with tabula-rasa symbolic regression}

Modern symbolic regression (SR) engines -- PySR~\cite{cranmer2023},
AI Feynman~\cite{udrescu2020,udrescu2020b}, PhySO~\cite{tenachi2023},
Eureqa~\cite{schmidt2009} -- are powerful tools for recovering algebraic
equations from data.
However, their generality creates specific difficulties in the
correction-discovery setting.

\textbf{Catastrophic cancellation at classical limits.}
A correction $\Delta$ satisfies $\Delta \to 0$ at the classical limit.
For a general-purpose optimizer reconstructing $1 + \Delta$ near that limit,
gradients with respect to $\Delta$ become vanishingly small.
In IEEE-754 \texttt{float32} arithmetic~\cite{ieee754} (used by many GPU
pipelines), evaluating the naive Lorentz expression $(1-u)^{-1/2} - 1$ at
$u = 10^{-8}$ returns exactly 0.0 due to floating-point subtraction.
Even in \texttt{float64}, cancellation begins to degrade precision near
$u \approx 10^{-12}$ (relative error $\sim 10^{-4}$) and collapses to exactly
0.0 at $u \approx 10^{-16}$.

\textbf{No identifiability reporting.}
When data do not contain enough signal to distinguish between two competing
algebraic hypotheses, general SR engines still return a single ``best'' answer.
There is no built-in mechanism to declare: \emph{these data cannot determine
whether the correction is Lorentzian or rational; more data are needed.}
The benchmark study of La Cava et al.~\cite{lacava2021} documents this
empirically across contemporary SR methods.

\textbf{Non-determinism.}
Evolutionary and Monte Carlo based search is stochastic by design~\cite{bongard2007}.
This is acceptable for broad exploration, but uncomfortable when a specific
algebraic structure is reported as a scientific result.

\subsection{Our approach}

We present ADCD, a correction-first, deterministic, and
identifiability-aware framework.
Its core commitments are:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Correction-first formulation.} The classical baseline is known;
        ADCD searches only for the residual correction $\Delta$, constrained to
        vanish at the classical limit.
  \item \textbf{Algebraically regularized primitives.} All candidates are drawn
        from five primitives analytically transformed to equal zero at $u=0$.
  \item \textbf{Deterministic enumeration.} Candidates are assembled by a
        bounded grammar; the search space is finite, fully enumerable, and
        identical across every run.
  \item \textbf{Physical gating before fitting.} Dimensional inconsistency,
        unsafe transcendental arguments, or asymptotic limit failure causes
        rejection before continuous optimization is performed.
  \item \textbf{Explicit identifiability reporting.} Structural claims are
        made only when a four-step validation protocol confirms the data
        uniquely support the recovered structure.
\end{enumerate}

This paper describes the framework, its mathematical foundation, and its
validation on three canonical synthetic physics scenarios.
We state clearly what the framework can and cannot currently do.

\section{Related Work}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{General symbolic regression.}
Eureqa~\cite{schmidt2009} pioneered automated law discovery using evolutionary
search.
PySR~\cite{cranmer2023} uses tournament evolutionary search and has become a
standard benchmark tool.
AI Feynman~\cite{udrescu2020,udrescu2020b} exploits neural network
decomposition of symmetries, achieving strong results on the Feynman
database~\cite{lacava2021}.
PhySO~\cite{tenachi2023} incorporates dimensional analysis constraints via
deep reinforcement learning.
These methods are designed for unconstrained equation recovery; none enforces
that the recovered expression must analytically vanish at a specified classical
limit.

\textbf{Sparse regression and SINDy.}
Brunton et al.~\cite{brunton2016} demonstrated data-driven discovery of
governing equations using sparse regression (SINDy), operating in a
user-specified library of candidate terms.
ADCD shares the library-based philosophy but targets a different problem:
recovering corrections to known laws, with algebraic enforcement of the
classical-limit constraint.

\textbf{Physics-informed learning.}
Raissi et al.~\cite{raissi2019} introduced physics-informed neural networks
(PINNs) for embedding physical laws as soft constraints.
Karniadakis et al.~\cite{karniadakis2021} and Cornelio et al.~\cite{cornelio2023}
extended this line toward integrating hard symbolic constraints with data.
ADCD complements this direction by imposing hard constraints algebraically,
not as loss penalties.

\textbf{Physics-informed constraints in SR.}
Dimensional analysis has been applied as a filter in~\cite{tenachi2023,petersen2021}.
ADCD applies dimensional analysis not as a post-hoc filter but as a hard gate
before numerical fitting, and extends this to transcendental argument safety
and asymptotic limit verification.

\textbf{Model selection for SR.}
The Bayesian Information Criterion~\cite{schwarz1978}, interpreted under the
Kass--Raftery scale~\cite{kass1995}, provides a principled tool for comparing
models of differing complexity.
We use BIC as the primary identifiability metric and require a minimum
$\Delta\mathrm{BIC} > 10$ (very strong evidence) before any structural claim.

\textbf{Correction-specific discovery.}
To the best of our knowledge, no existing SR framework explicitly targets the
correction-first paradigm with algebraically enforced classical-limit constraints
and built-in identifiability boundaries.

\section{Methods}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Problem formulation}

Let $y_\mathrm{obs}$ be the observed target and $y_\mathrm{cl}$ the known
classical prediction.
Define the correction residual:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta =
  \begin{cases}
    y_\mathrm{obs}/y_\mathrm{cl} - 1 & \text{(multiplicative)} \\
    y_\mathrm{obs} - y_\mathrm{cl}   & \text{(additive)}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The multiplicative formulation is adopted when its residual shows weaker Pearson
correlation with the classical prediction magnitude than the
additive residual does.
ADCD searches for $\hat{\Delta}(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\theta})$, where
$\mathbf{x}$ is the vector of physical inputs, $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ are
continuous free parameters, and $\hat{\Delta} \to 0$ as the relevant
dimensionless parameter $u \to 0$.

\subsection{Regularized primitive dictionary}

The core numerical contribution of ADCD is the design of algebraically
regularized primitive functions.
Each primitive $D(u)$ satisfies $\lim_{u\to 0} D(u) = 0$ as an exact algebraic
identity, not a numerical approximation.
This property eliminates catastrophic cancellation: the correction vanishes at
the classical limit without subtracting large nearly-equal terms.

Table~\ref{tab:primitives} lists the five primitives.
The practical consequence is demonstrated for \texttt{D\_lor}: in
\texttt{float32}, the naive form $(1-u)^{-1/2} - 1$ collapses to exactly 0.0
at $u = 10^{-8}$; in \texttt{float64}, precision degrades progressively
(11\% relative error at $u = 10^{-15}$; total collapse at $u = 10^{-16}$).
The regularized form $u\,/\,[\sqrt{1-u}(\sqrt{1-u}+1)]$ preserves full
precision at all scales, returning $5.0 \times 10^{-17}$ at $u = 10^{-16}$
where the naive form returns 0.0.

$\lim_{u\to0} D(u) = 0$ as an exact algebraic identity.}
\label{tab:primitives}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Name & Regularized form $D(u)$ & Domain \\
\midrule
\texttt{D\_lor}      & $u\,/\,[\sqrt{1-u}(\sqrt{1-u}+1)]$ & $u \in [0,1)$ \\
\texttt{D\_rat}      & $u\,/\,(1-u)$                       & $u \neq 1$ \\
\texttt{D\_exp}      & $e^{-u} - 1$                        & all $u$ \\

\end{tabular}

\subsection{Grammar enumeration and the theta-intact constraint}

Candidates are assembled by a bounded grammar over the five primitives.
A candidate takes the form $\hat{\Delta} = \theta_0 \cdot D_i(u_{ij})$, where
$u_{ij}$ is a dimensionless ratio of input variables and scale parameters.
The grammar is restricted to a maximum composition depth of 3 (up to 3 nested primitive layers) and token count $\leq 25$.

All structural gates (Section~\ref{sec:gates}) are evaluated on the
\emph{theta-intact} expression -- the fully parameterized form before any
$\theta_i$ are substituted with probe values.
Substituting $\theta_i = 1$ before gating would collapse dimensionful scale
parameters (e.g., $r/\theta_1$, where $\theta_1$ represents the Debye length)
into dimensionless constants, falsifying dimensional analysis.
In practice, the AST gate alone yields a uniform $0.56$ survival rate across
scenarios; overall survival (after all five gates) is $0.56$, $0.52$, and $0.52$
for TD, SC, and EE respectively, reflecting a known and deliberate trade-off
of honest structural assessment.
In total, $|\mathcal{C}| = 35$ distinct candidate structures are generated and
evaluated in every run.

\subsection{Physical gate cascade}
\label{sec:gates}

Before numerical fitting, each candidate passes five gates in ascending
computational cost.

\textbf{Gate~1 (AST complexity).}
Candidates whose resulting SymPy expression tree exceeds depth 9 or token count $>25$
(theta-intact) are rejected.

\textbf{Gate~2 (Dimensional consistency).}
Physical dimensions are tracked as integer vectors in the SI base.
Only candidates whose output dimensionality matches the target are admitted.

\textbf{Gate~3 (Transcendental safety).}
Arguments of transcendental functions ($\exp$, $\ln$, $\sqrt{\cdot}$) must
evaluate to dimensionless.
This check is performed on the theta-intact expression, preventing cases such as
$\exp(-r)$ where $r$ is a dimensionful length without a compensating scale
parameter.

\textbf{Gate~4 (Asymptotic regime check, ARC).}
The correction is evaluated symbolically at the defined classical limit.
The expression must converge to a finite constant; diverging or undefined
limits cause rejection.

\textbf{Gate~5 (Coarse numerical fitness).}
The candidate is evaluated against the dataset with
$\boldsymbol{\theta} = \mathbf{1}$ as a computational pre-screen.
Candidates producing non-finite outputs are rejected via
$\texttt{np.isfinite(mse)}$, correctly catching both \texttt{inf} and
\texttt{NaN} values.

\subsection{Parameter optimization}

Candidates surviving all five gates are optimized via L-BFGS-B, implemented
in JAX with \texttt{float64} precision.
All free parameters are log-parameterized
($\theta_i = s_i e^{u_i}$, with $s_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ preserving the sign of the
initial value) to allow traversal across orders of magnitude with smooth gradients.
After fitting, a post-fit ARC re-verification repeats the asymptotic check
with $\boldsymbol{\theta} = \boldsymbol{\theta}^*$, confirming the fitted
parameters do not move the expression into a non-asymptotic regime.

\subsection{Model selection via BIC}

The final model is selected by the Bayesian Information Criterion~\cite{schwarz1978}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BIC} = k \ln N - 2 \ln \hat{L}
\end{equation}
where $k$ is the number of free parameters, $N$ the data size, and $\hat{L}$
the maximized likelihood under Gaussian noise.
By the Kass--Raftery scale~\cite{kass1995}, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} > 10$
constitutes very strong evidence for the preferred model.
We use this as the minimum threshold for any structural claim.

\subsection{Four-step validation protocol}

A structural claim is made only when all four checks pass simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Budget disclosure.} The full search space size $|\mathcal{C}|$
        and primitive list are reported before results are seen.
  \item \textbf{Positive control.} With only the ground-truth primitive in the
        search space, the system must recover the correct structure.
  \item \textbf{Ablation control.} With the ground-truth primitive excluded,
        the BIC of the best alternative must differ from the correct structure
        by $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} > 10$.
  \item \textbf{Determinism check.} Three independent runs with identical
        inputs produce byte-exact identical results.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Experimental setup}

We evaluate ADCD on three canonical synthetic physics scenarios.
In each, synthetic observations are generated by applying a known correction to
a classical law and adding 1\% Gaussian multiplicative noise
($y_\mathrm{obs} = y_\mathrm{true}(1 + \epsilon)$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, 0.01)$),

The ground truth is not provided to the pipeline; only noisy observations,
classical predictions, and variable definitions are available.

These are \emph{rediscovery} experiments: the ground truth is known and
used to verify structural recovery.
We make no claim of discovering previously unknown physics.

\section{Discussion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Explicit scope of claims}

We state precisely what this paper claims and what it does not.

\textbf{Claimed:} For three multiplicative correction scenarios, ADCD
deterministically recovers the correct algebraic structure from 1\%-noisy
synthetic data.
All three pass the full four-step validation protocol with
$\Delta\mathrm{BIC} \geq 59.6$.
Results are byte-exact reproducible.
The framework explicitly reports identifiability confidence rather than
returning a structural claim without qualification.

\textbf{Not claimed:} General-purpose equation discovery.
Discovery of previously unknown physics.
Performance superiority over general SR engines for arbitrary problems.
Robustness to corrections outside the five-primitive dictionary.
Performance on additive correction scenarios.

\subsection{Comparison with general SR}

ADCD and general SR engines address different problems.
General SR is designed to recover full governing equations from scratch.
ADCD is designed for a specific sub-problem: given a known classical baseline,
recover the structured correction that vanishes at the classical limit.

The meaningful comparison is not which tool answers faster, but which provides
stronger identifiability guarantees.
General SR engines do not report whether data are theoretically sufficient to
discriminate between competing hypotheses.
ADCD does, at the cost of being restricted to a bounded grammar.

\subsection{The Screened Coulomb identifiability boundary}

The Screened Coulomb $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = 59.64$ is the smallest of the three,
and worth discussing explicitly.
The next-best competing structure achieves NMSE $= 3.1 \times 10^{-4}$ compared
to the correct $2.8 \times 10^{-4}$, a difference of only 11\%.
At higher noise levels, we expect $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}$ to drop below the
10-unit threshold, at which point ADCD would withhold the structural claim.
This behavior -- declining confidence with declining signal -- is the intended
mode of the identifiability protocol.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{Dictionary restriction.}
The five primitives cover the most common correction types in classical-to-modern
physics.
Physical corrections outside this dictionary cannot be represented.
Extension is architecturally straightforward but requires held-out validation
of each new primitive before incorporation.

\textbf{Multiplicative corrections only (current validation).}
The validation protocol has been applied and confirmed only for multiplicative
correction scenarios.
Additive corrections require solving for the physical dimensions of the
correction term, an open problem not addressed here.

\textbf{Synthetic data only.}
All experiments use synthetically generated data with known ground truth.
Real observational data introduces correlated noise, systematic errors, and the
possibility that the true correction lies outside the five-primitive dictionary.

\textbf{Rediscovery, not blind discovery.}
All three validated scenarios involve corrections whose algebraic form is
already known from physics literature.
The framework recovers these forms from data.
We do not claim to have discovered previously unknown corrections.

\subsection{Future Work}

\textbf{Simplified input interface.}
The current interface requires users to specify dimensionless ratio candidates
explicitly (e.g.\ \texttt{ratio\_symbol="r/theta\_1"}), which demands
familiarity with the asymptotic grammar and is a known source of configuration
error.
A planned follow-up engineering contribution is a rule-based wrapper
(\texttt{adcd.quickfit()}) that automatically enumerates dimensionless ratio
candidates from user-supplied variable definitions using dimensional analysis
(Buckingham~$\pi$ theorem).
This wrapper is purely deterministic -- no learned components, no external
calls -- while leaving the core four-step validation protocol entirely
unchanged.
It would be validated independently against held-out test cases before release,
and would not alter any claim made in this paper.

\textbf{Primitive dictionary expansion.}
Additional primitives (e.g.\ polynomial, oscillatory, or logarithmic
corrections) can be incorporated once held-out validation confirms asymptotic
regularity and classical-limit identifiability, following the same protocol
applied to the five current primitives.

\textbf{Real observational data.}
Extending validation to real experimental datasets requires handling correlated
noise, systematic offsets, and the possibility that the true correction lies
outside the current dictionary.
Identifiability analysis must be re-run per dataset class before any structural
claim is made.

\section{Conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

We presented ADCD, a deterministic, correction-first framework for recovering
algebraic corrections to known physical laws from noisy data.
Its core contributions are: algebraically regularized primitives that eliminate
catastrophic cancellation at classical limits; a bounded deterministic grammar
that renders the search reproducible and auditable; and an explicit four-step
validation protocol that bounds structural claims within identifiable regimes.

On three canonical scenarios (special-relativistic time dilation, Yukawa plasma
screening, and ideal-gas isothermal expansion), ADCD recovers the correct
algebraic correction structure with NMSE below $3.4 \times 10^{-3}$, BIC
differences of 59.6--2024 over the best competing structure, and byte-exact
determinism across repeated runs.

The framework is deliberately scoped.
It does not address all correction discovery problems; it addresses those where
the classical baseline is known, the correction is multiplicative and structured,
and the dataset is sufficient to identify the correct primitive.
Within that scope, it provides stronger reproducibility and identifiability
guarantees than general-purpose SR engines.

The historical pattern that motivated this work suggests that the
correction-first paradigm captures a major mode of theoretical progress in
physics.
Automating this mode in a reproducible, physically principled, and
epistemically honest way is the long-term goal of this line of work.
